# Mammoth Sea Battles: Rulebook

> **Playtest draft 0.2**, written for players from the design spec ([`rules.md`](rules.md) v0.111).
> Covers the **core game**, **Treasure Hunt**, **Race** and **teams**. Add-ons are in the appendix.
> 🛠 marks rules still being decided.

---

## Welcome aboard

The seas are full of treasure, and every pirate crew of mammoths and prehistoric monsters wants it. Program your ship's orders in
secret, then watch everything happen at once: ships collide, currents sweep them off course, the wind shoves them about, and the
cannons roar. Plans go wrong. That's the fun.

**Players:** 2–8 · **Win:** be first to bring **3 treasures** home to your port (2 with 7–8 players).

## 1. What's in the box

| Component | Use |

| 8 ships | One per player (or two per player in Fleets, §12) |
| 8 ship boards | Where you program your orders |
| Main boards (large hexagonal boards) | Build the sea |
| 8 port tiles (4 hexes each) | Your home port |
| Command cards (one shared deck) | Your orders: Sail / Steer / Action |
| Crew cards | Captains (mammoths) and crew (prehistoric monsters) |
| Weather board + weather cards | Wind, weather and tides |
| Treasure tokens + treasure map deck (1–9) | Where treasure appears |

| Shipwreck tokens | Searchable wrecks |
| Damage markers | Block slots on your ship board |
| 12-sided damage dice | Damage |

## 2. Setting up

1. **Build the sea together.** Join **main boards** (big hexagonal boards) into a layout everyone likes: the host decides, or take turns.
   Try one of the suggested layouts for your first game 🛠 *(to come)*. Usually **2–4 boards**: fewer boards make a tight, combat-heavy
   game; more boards make more of a race. More ships need more boards.
2. Each player (or team) takes a **port tile** (4 hexes) and attaches it to the **outer edge** of the map. It can face any way round, but
   **sea hex must join a sea hex**, and **no two ports may share the same side** of a main board. Each player takes a **ship** and
   **ship board**.
3. Place the **9 spawn point tokens** and **one shipwreck token per ship**: agree where they go, or take turns placing them. Spawn points can all be at sea (a quick
   game), all on land (a longer game), or a mix.
4. Shuffle the **treasure map deck**. Turn over cards and place treasure on those spawn points until there are
   **half as many treasures as ships** (rounded down, minimum 1): 1 treasure with 2–3 ships, 2 with 4–5, 3 with 6–7, 4 with 8.
5. Each player takes a **Captain** and **2 random crew**, face up in front of them.
6. Fill the **weather board** with weather cards. The current card and the **next one** are face up. The starting tide is random.
7. Ships start **in their home port** with **sail down (No Sail)**, pointing whichever way their player chooses.
8. Each player draws **command cards**: one for each card icon on their healthy crew (§4).

---

## 3. Your ship

### Heading and the hex grid
Your ship always points at one of the **six sides** of her hex. She moves forward through that side.

### Sail
Your ship's sail is set to **No Sail**, **Half Sail** or **Full Sail**. It **stays set** until you change it. You don't need to play a
sail card every time.

### Cannons
Your cannons are on your ship's **sides (broadsides)**. Each side fires into a **60° wedge** out to **4 hexes**. You can't fire straight
ahead or straight behind. (Other weapons only come from crew and equipment.)

---

## 4. Command cards and your hand

Every command card has **three bands**:

- 🟦 **Sail** (top): No Sail, Half Sail or Full Sail
- 🟨 **Steer** (middle): Ease Port or Ease Starboard (turn 60°), Hard to Port or Hard to Starboard (turn 120°)
- 🟥 **Action** (bottom): Fire Cannons, Repair or Heal

Each card also has an **initiative number** (top right). Higher numbers act first when it matters.

**Your hand:** at Upkeep, draw **1 card for each card icon on your healthy crew** (a Captain gives 3, most crew give 1; **wounded crew
give none**). Keep cards you don't use. Your hand is secret. **Hand limit 9:** after programming, discard down to 9 if you need to.

**Used cards** are gathered, roughly shuffled, and put on the **bottom of the deck**.

---

## 5. Playing a turn

Each turn has three stages: **Program → Resolve Parts I, II and III → Upkeep.**

### 5.1 Program (everyone at once, in secret)
Your ship board has **three Parts (I, II, III)**, and each Part has three slots: **Sail, Steer, Action**.

- Place cards face down in any slots, **up to 9 cards**.
- **Only the band matching the slot counts.** The same card could be a Full Sail order, a Hard to Port order, or a Fire Cannons order,
  depending on where you put it.
- **Empty slots are fine:** an empty Sail slot keeps your sail as it is; an empty Steer slot means no turn; an empty Action slot means no action.

### 5.2 Resolve each Part
Do Parts I, II and III in order. At the start of each Part, **reveal that Part's cards**. Then **everyone acts at the same time** in these
phases:

| | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | **Sail** | Set your sail if you played a Sail card. Then sail forward (§6). |
| **b** | **Steer** | Turn as your Steer card says. |
| **c** | **Currents** | Currents carry and turn ships (§7). |
| **d** | **Action** | Fire, Repair or Heal (§8). Then home ports fire. |
| **e** | **Wind drift** | In strong winds, ships drift 1 hex downwind (§6.4). |
| **f** | **End of Part** | Ships in rough water or storms take damage (§7). |

**When ships get in each other's way**, use **initiative**: the higher number goes first, then the other.
- For **movement**, use the number on the card in your **Sail** slot.
- For **firing**, use the number on the card in your **Action** slot.
- An **empty** slot counts as **0**. On a tie, roll off.

### 5.3 Upkeep
🛠 *Playtest order:*
1. **Sunk ships come back** (§9).
2. **Refit** ships that ended the turn in their home port (§10).
3. **Search wrecks** (§9).
4. **New treasure** appears for each one scored this turn (§11).
5. **Gale damage:** in a Gale, each ship takes **1 damage die for each Part she sailed at Full Sail** (§6.2).
6. **Advance the weather board** one space: new wind, new tide. Ships caught by the tide are now stranded, or freed (§7).
7. **Draw** command cards (§4), knowing the new weather.

*(With the Sea Monster add-on, it moves first.)*

## 6. Sailing and the wind

### 6.1 How far you sail
How far you sail depends on your **sail** and the **wind**. Find the wind arrow on the current weather card. **The wind blows the way the arrow
points.** Then look at your ship's heading (at the start of the Sail phase) compared with the wind:

| Your heading | Half Sail | Full Sail |
|---|---|---|
| **Into the wind** | don't move | **1 hex backwards** (taken aback!) |
| **Close hauled** (60° off the wind) | 1 | 2 |
| **Broad reach** (wind behind your side) | **2** | **3**, the fastest |
| **Running** (wind straight behind) | 1 | 2 |

No Sail never moves.

> **Simpler rule** (optional, for first games): ignore the wind direction. Half Sail moves 1, Full Sail moves 2.

### 6.2 Weather changes your speed

| Weather | Effect on sailing | Drift |
|---|---|---|
| **Dead Calm** | Nobody sails | None |
| **Light** | −1 to all sail moves | None |
| **Firm** | Normal | Part III |
| **Strong** | +1 to Full Sail moves | Parts II and III |
| **Gale** | +1 to all sail moves; **1 damage die for each Part sailed at Full Sail**, taken at Upkeep | Parts I, II and III |

Bonuses and penalties change the **number of hexes**, whichever way you're going, but never below 0. Into the wind at Full Sail in a Gale,
you're blown **2 hexes backwards**!

### 6.3 Pushing and ramming
If you sail into another ship, **you push her** ahead of you, hex for hex, as far as you keep moving. She can push the next ship, and so on.
The **first ship you hit takes 1 damage die**. A ship pushed into rocks, reefs or anything else suffers exactly as if she'd sailed there.

Only sailing pushes. **Currents and drift never push**: if they'd carry a ship into another one, she just stays put.

### 6.4 Wind drift
In the Parts shown above, every ship (even at No Sail) drifts **1 hex downwind**, without turning.

## 7. The sea

All of these work **however your ship got there**: sailing, pushed, carried by a current or drifting.

| Feature | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Cliffs** (purple rock) | Stop. Take **2 damage dice**. |
| **Beach** (yellow sand) | Stop. |
| **Sandbank** (sand over water) | Like a beach, **except at High tide**, when it's water. |
| **Underwater reef** (green stripes) | Water, **except at Low tide**, when it's like a beach. |
| **Death reef** (skull) | Your ship **sinks**. |
| **Wall reef** (spiky edge between hexes) | Can't cross it. Stop and take **2 damage dice**. |
| **Rough water** | Take **2 damage dice** if you're in it at the end of a Part. |
| **Storm** | Take **3 damage dice** if you're in it at the end of a Part. 🛠 *Provisional.* |
| **Fog** | Ships in fog can't fire or be fired at. You can't fire through fog. |
| **Map edge** | A ship that would leave the map **sinks** in the hex she was about to leave. |

**Stop** means you stay in the last hex and lose the rest of that move. Currents, pushes and drift can still move you later.

### Currents
Arrows move ships in the Currents phase. **Single arrows move 1 hex, double 2, triple 3**; curved arrows also **turn** you. Circular arrows
**spin** you on the spot. 🛠 *A diagram of every current type is coming.*

Currents move in three steps: first only triple currents move 1 hex, then double and triple currents, then all currents. This way a ship can
be carried from one current onto the next.

### Tides
The weather board shows the tide, which cycles **High → Mid → Mid → Low → Mid → Mid** over six turns. You can always see what's coming.

| Tide | Sandbanks | Underwater reefs |
|---|---|---|
| High | water | water |
| Mid | land | water |
| Low | land | land |

**Stranded!** If the tide turns your hex to land, your ship is **stuck** until the water returns. You can't sail, steer, be pushed or drift,
but you **can still take Actions**. Other ships treat your hex as beach.

**Haul her out:** a stranded ship can spend a **whole turn** to move **1 hex**, hopefully into water.

### Whirlpools and caves
- **Whirlpools** come in numbered pairs. Move into one and you **pop out of its partner** with the same heading, carrying on with any movement
  left. If the partner is occupied, you just carry on across. Arriving through a whirlpool doesn't send you back.
- **Sea caves** are lettered pairs on island edges. Move into one (even backwards or sideways) and you come out of its partner **facing out
  of the cave mouth**, carrying on with any movement left. If a ship is in the way, you push her.
- Cannons can't fire through whirlpools or caves.

## 8. Actions and combat

Actions happen in **initiative order** (Action slot number, highest first), and damage takes effect immediately. **A ship sunk before her
turn doesn't get to fire.**

- **Fire Cannons**: both broadsides fire. **Every enemy ship in either arc takes 1 damage die**; other ships don't shield them. You may choose
  to spare any of them. Cannons fire over land, but not into, out of or through fog. No friendly fire.
- **Repair**: remove 1 damage marker, from any slot.
- **Heal**: flip 1 wounded crew member back to healthy. (Heal can't bring back dead crew.)
- **Home ports** then fire at every rival ship within 2 hexes: 1 damage die each.

### Damage dice
Roll a 12-sided die for each damage die. **You** decide where the damage goes.

| Roll | Ship damage | Crew damage |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | – | – (miss!) |
| 2–3 | 1 | – |
| 4–5 | – | 1 |
| 6–7 | 1 | 1 |
| 8 | 2 | – |
| 9 | – | 2 |
| 10 | 1 | 2 |
| 11 | 2 | 1 |
| 12 | **Critical hit!** Roll this die again **and** one extra die. 🛠 *Critical cards may replace this.* |

- **Ship damage**: put a **damage marker** on any slot of your ship board. Remove any card there. That slot can't be used until repaired.
  (A marker on a slot whose Part has already happened just blocks it from next turn.)
- **Crew damage**: **wound** a healthy crew member (flip the card) or **kill** a wounded one. Your **Captain** can be wounded and killed too.
  Wounded crew give no cards, and their skills stop working until they're healed.

**Your ship sinks** if a whole row of slots (all Sail, all Steer or all Action) is blocked, or if you have **no healthy crew**.

Damage mostly **slows you down**: fewer slots and fewer cards mean fewer choices.

## 9. Sinking and shipwrecks

**If your ship sinks:**
- Any treasure she carried **drops where she sank**.
- She leaves a **shipwreck token** there.
- At **Upkeep** she **comes back at your home port**, fully repaired, with **3 random crew**. **Your Captain is lost.** Choose her heading;
  her sail is down. If the port hex is occupied, she comes back next to it.
- She **discards her hand** and draws a **new hand** from her new crew. She **plays the next turn as normal**. You're never out of the game.

**Searching wrecks:** at Upkeep, a ship that ended the turn **on a wreck** (or next to it, if it's on land) searches it, **gains a crew
card**, and removes the wreck. A ship can have **at most 6 crew**: if you're full, you may swap one out.

There's a **Retired Captain** somewhere in the crew deck: the best crew member of all, but she won't serve alongside another Captain.

## 10. Home port

- **Start:** you begin the game here.
- **Your port hex** is the port tile's only sea hex. It's the only part of the tile a ship can enter.
- **Safe harbour:** your port can't be harmed, and its guns fire at rivals within 2 hexes.
- **Any port in a storm:** while your ship is in port, **no weather effects of any kind** happen to her: no drift, no storm damage, no Gale damage.
  Even a Dead Calm can't keep you in port: you always get out to the hex outside, and then the weather takes over.
- **Refit:** end a turn in your port to fully repair, fully heal, and recruit back up to 3 crew (drawn at random). You can do just part of a
  refit, but either way you **miss the next turn** (you still draw cards).
- **Score:** sail into your port carrying treasure to score it. It's safe forever. You can only score at your own port.

## 11. Treasure Hunt

- **Picking up:** sail **through** a treasure's sea hex to grab it. For treasure **on land**, end a turn **next to it**: you've gone ashore to dig.
- **Carry 1** treasure at a time.
- **Stealing:** when you **hit or ram** a ship carrying treasure, you may give up that damage die and **knock the treasure loose** instead.
  Place it in a hex next to her, sea or land (reefs and sandbanks are fine). The hex can't already hold treasure, but it can hold a ship.
- **Scoring:** enter your home port with it.
- **New treasure:** at Upkeep, turn over a treasure map card for each treasure scored that turn. If that spawn point already has a treasure,
  draw again; if every spawn point has treasure, skip it. A ship on the spawn point can grab it straight away. Reshuffle the deck when it
  runs out.
- **Winning:** first to **3** (2 with 7–8 players; with 2 players, play best of 5, i.e. first to 3).
- **Timed game (variant):** play to a timer (e.g. 2 hours); finish the current turn when it runs out. Most treasure scored wins.
  🛠 *Tie-breaks to playtest: carrying treasure, then nearest to home port, then sudden death.*

## 12. Teams and Fleets

**Teams:** players can play in teams (for example 4 players in 2 teams of 2). Teammates:
- **share one home port**, and **share goals** (treasure scored by any teammate counts for the team; in Race, a checkpoint only needs reaching
  once per team);
- **talk openly**, and may show each other their hands and programs;
- **never hit each other**, and ramming a teammate deals **no damage die**;
- **pass treasure** by ramming: knock your teammate's treasure loose (no damage die) and pick it up.

**Fleets (expert variant):** each player commands **two ships**, which form a team. Best for 2 players (4 ships). Each ship has her own
ship board, crew (a Captain + 2 each), hand, damage and initiative. Hard work, but brilliant.

---

## 13. Other ways to play

- **Race:** checkpoints are agreed at setup. Pass through them **in order**; the first ship (or team) through the last one wins. A sunk ship
  comes back at her **last checkpoint**. The simplest way to learn.
- **Deathmatch** 🛠 *(to be designed)*: usually in teams.
- **Capture the Flag** 🛠 *(to be designed)*: teams.
- **Solo / Co-op Adventure** 🛠: explore and fight ghost ships and monsters. Coming later.

## Appendix: add-ons

Add these once the core game is familiar.

**Crew skills.** Many crew have special skills. For example the **Surgeon** heals one wounded crew member each turn for free.

**Equipment.** Single-use cards, kept secret, played at set moments. For example **Explosive cannon balls** add +2 damage dice to an attack.
There's no limit on how many you hold, and you keep them if you sink. With this add-on, searching a wreck gives a crew **or** an equipment card.

**Flotsam.** Floating wreckage that gives equipment:
- **Battle debris:** whenever a ship takes **Ship damage** (from anything), put a flotsam token in the hex **directly behind** her. If that hex
  is blocked, no flotsam.
- **Weather:** a weather card with the **flotsam icon** brings one token. The player in last place puts it in an empty sea hex **more than 5
  hexes from every ship** (if there's no such hex, no flotsam).
- **Picking up:** any ship that moves over flotsam takes it and draws an **equipment card**. Flotsam doesn't drift.
- **Pool:** only **5** flotsam tokens; when they're all on the board, no more appear until one is picked up.

**The Derelict.** An abandoned ship full of loot:
- **Arrives:** a **Dead Calm** card announces it; it arrives with the **next weather card that has wind**. The player in last place puts it
  in an empty hex on that wind's **upwind edge**.
- **Drifts** like a ship at No Sail (currents and wind). Sailing into it pushes it.
- **Boarding:** end a turn next to it to loot it: **2 equipment cards + 1 treasure** (the treasure only if your ship has room, and only if
  the maximum treasures aren't already in play). Several boarders take the loot one item at a time, in initiative order. Then it's removed.
- **Sinking it:** it sinks after **4 damage dice** and leaves flotsam. It's also lost if it drifts off the map or onto a death reef.

**The Sea Monster.**
- Each turn at Upkeep it moves 1 hex. The player with the most **🐙 influence** chooses where: 🐙 icons on the cards they programmed this
  turn, plus each damage die they hit it with last turn. On a tie, the closest ship decides.
- After each Action phase it deals 1 damage die to every ship next to it.
- Any ship that enters its hex, or that it moves onto, **sinks**.
- It ignores the map, and can be shot but never killed.

## Quick reference

**Each Part:** reveal → Sail → Steer → Currents → Action → Drift → End of Part.
**Initiative:** Sail slot for moving, Action slot for firing; higher first; empty = 0; ties roll off.
**Upkeep:** sunk ships return → refit → search wrecks → new treasure → Gale damage → weather and tide → draw.
**Sink when:** a whole row of slots is blocked, or no healthy crew.
