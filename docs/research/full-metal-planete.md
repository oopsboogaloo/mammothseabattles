# Reference: Full Metal Planète (Ludodélire, 1988): tides

Notes on the tide mechanic only. Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Metal_Plan%C3%A8te),
[RulesPal rulebook](https://rulespal.com/full-metal-planete/rulebook),
[rules summary](https://lehi-innovation.github.io/boardgame-rules/rules/full-metal-planete/). These are secondary sources; the
official rulebook hasn't been checked.

- **Tide deck**: 15 tide cards are shuffled face down. Each turn a tide card sets the tide to **Low, Normal or High**.
- **Two tidal terrain types**, marsh (swamp) and reef. Plain land and open sea never change.

| Tide | Marsh | Reef |
|---|---|---|
| Low | land | land |
| Normal | land | sea |
| High | sea | sea |

- **Stranding**: a unit on terrain that the tide makes wrong for it is **neutralised** (can't act) until a favourable
  tide returns. Sea units are stuck on marsh/reef at low tide, and on marsh at normal tide.
- **Forecast**: players with a working weather layer can look at the **next** tide card. Information about the future is a
  resource.

## Lessons for Mammoth Sea Battles
- Two tidal terrain types with staggered thresholds give 3 board states using only 2 printed terrain types.
- The map changes, but every hex shows what it can become, so it's predictable.
- Getting stranded is a real risk, and it's part of the fun.
